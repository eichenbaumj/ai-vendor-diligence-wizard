# Competitive & Partner Landscape: AI Vendor Diligence Wizard

Research date: August 28, 2026. All claims below are sourced from live fetches/searches performed today; where the public record ran out, uncertainty is flagged explicitly.

---

## 1. Does anything like this already exist?

**Short answer: No.** There is a crowded field of adjacent tools — vendor disclosure registries, peer-review platforms, security verification programs, procurement frameworks, readiness assessments, and training — but **nothing that takes an inbound pitch (email/PDF/URL/name) as input and returns an automated, evidence-based legitimacy evaluation with recommended next steps.** Every existing tool assumes the government has already decided to evaluate a vendor and has staff time to do it; none addresses the top-of-funnel triage problem. Detail by player:

### 1.1 GovAI Coalition — vendor AI FactSheets + AI Registry (closest conceptual neighbor)

- Founded October 2023 with nine founding members (San Jose, Cleveland, San Antonio, San Diego, St. Paul, Long Beach, San Diego County, TriMet, Colorado Dept. of Revenue); went public with deliverables March 13, 2024 ([TechPolicy.press](https://www.techpolicy.press/the-power-of-the-collective-purse-opensource-ai-governance-and-the-govai-coalition/)). Now claims **members from over 900 local, state, and federal agencies in seven countries** ([GovAI Coalition Summit 2026 page](https://events.govtech.com/GovAI-Coalition-Summit); summit is Dec 9–10, 2026, San Jose Convention Center, co-hosted by City of San José and Government Technology).
- Its **Vendor AI FactSheet** is an "AI nutrition label": system overview, training data sources and update procedures, model architecture, performance metrics, bias/fairness mitigation, robustness, WCAG accessibility, human oversight ([Polimorphic's completed FactSheet example](https://www.polimorphic.com/blog-posts/govai-coalition-vendor-fact-sheet), Dec 2024). The Coalition maintains a **vendor registry** — a shared repository of FactSheets functioning as an "unofficial pre-qualification list" — with a dedicated **Vendor Registry Committee** among its seven committees ([ICMA blog](https://icma.org/blog-posts/govai-coalition-free-membership-and-committee-access)).
- The registry is hosted on **Hortus AI's "Trellis" platform** (CEO Thomas Krendl Gilbert, PhD UC Berkeley), where vendors self-submit FactSheets ([San Jose GovAI pages](https://www.sanjoseca.gov/your-government/departments-offices/information-technology/ai-reviews-algorithm-register/govai-coalition), per search snippets; trellis.hortus.ai and hortus.ai both returned 403 to direct fetch). **I am uncertain about the current number of FactSheets in the registry — no public count was found anywhere**, including the TechPolicy.press deep-dive and the 2026 summit page (which does not mention the registry at all — possibly a signal of low salience). Coverage appears vendor-participation-dependent and skewed toward Coalition-engaged vendors.
- The Coalition also has a **Contract Hub on Pavilion** ([withpavilion.com/associations/gov-ai](https://www.withpavilion.com/associations/gov-ai)) for cooperative contracts.
- **Gaps vs. our tool:** (a) requires the vendor to voluntarily complete a FactSheet — a cold-emailing startup usually hasn't; (b) self-reported, unverified claims ("90% accuracy based on internal testing"); (c) no pitch-ingestion, no research, no recommendation; (d) built for governance/procurement stage, not inbound triage. **It is a partner and an evidence source, not a competitor**: "Has this vendor filed a GovAI FactSheet?" is a check our tool should run and report.

### 1.2 Procurated (peer reviews of supplier past performance)

- The largest supplier ratings/reviews platform built for the public sector — "Yelp for government suppliers" — founded 2019 by Pennsylvania's former Chief Procurement Officer; **80,000+ reviews written exclusively by vetted government professionals**; free for governments; OpenGov procurement integration; new "Canary" contract-performance-intelligence product on top of the review stream ([app.procurated.com](https://app.procurated.com/), [Spend Matters analysis](https://spendmatters.com/2023/02/06/procurated-vendor-analysis-supplier-ratings-and-reviews-solution-overview/), [Carahsoft listing](https://www.carahsoft.com/procurated)).
- **Gaps:** only covers vendors that already have government customers willing to review them — exactly the vendors that need triage least. Nothing AI-specific (no model, data, or governance questions). No triage or recommendation layer. **Also an evidence source, not a competitor** (presence/absence + rating is a legitimacy signal).

### 1.3 G2 / Capterra / TrustRadius

- Direct fetches were blocked (G2 category page 403). **I am uncertain about exact current category counts.** Based on the structure of these platforms, govtech coverage is thin and poorly fitted: reviews come predominantly from private-sector users, reviewer government-affiliation is not verified, and the categories that matter here (benefits eligibility systems, government call center AI, document processing for caseworkers) either don't exist or contain a handful of reviews. No AI-governance content, no procurement context, heavy vendor pay-to-play dynamics. Not a competitor for public-sector triage.

### 1.4 Govlaunch

- Free "wiki for local government innovation": product directory + government project write-ups, tagged and searchable, with verified local-gov users ([govlaunch.com](https://govlaunch.com/products/govlaunch), [GovTech profile](https://www.govtech.com/biz/Govlaunch-Building-a-Free-Innovation-Wiki-for-Government.html)). Passive/community-driven; no AI diligence, no evaluation, no triage. Useful as a "does any government actually use this vendor?" evidence source.

### 1.5 Marketplace.city / Civic Marketplace / CivStart

- **Marketplace.city** ("Data-Driven Procurement & Vendor Intelligence for Government") helps local governments "source, validate, and procure" technology with vendor comparisons and a repository of contracts, references, and pricing ([marketplace.city](https://marketplace.city/)).
- **Civic Marketplace + CivStart Ventures** announced a partnership (Nov 2025) formalizing "joint standards around risk disclosure, due diligence, and performance verification" for startups selling to local government ([Businesswire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251106756735/en/Civic-Marketplace-and-CivStart-Ventures-Announce-Strategic-Partnership-to-Accelerate-Local-Government-Procurement-from-Startups)).
- **CivStart the nonprofit is shutting down in 2026** after 7 years (56 startups accelerated, $152M raised by alumni); its State of GovTech 2026 conference (Sep 9–10, Montgomery County, MD) continues under for-profit CivStart Ventures ([Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/civics/civstart-nonprofit-govtech-accelerator-closing/), [GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/biz/gov-tech-incubator-civstart-will-shut-down-in-2026)).
- **Gaps:** these are curated marketplaces and accelerator pipelines — vendor-opt-in, discovery-oriented, partly commercially motivated. None ingests an arbitrary inbound pitch. Not free/open-source public goods.

### 1.6 StateUp

- Has pivoted away from its earlier US-relevant "StateUp 21" public-purpose-tech cohort work; the site now presents a research/strategy consultancy focused on **energy resilience, critical technologies, and information environments**, with recent work on UK–Italy cooperation and a European "Ponti Summit" ([stateup.co](https://stateup.co/)). Not a competitor.

### 1.7 Results for America / What Works Cities

- **Economic Mobility Catalog**: 50+ strategies, ~200 evidence-rated practices and programs for local governments ([catalog.results4america.org](https://catalog.results4america.org/about)); **What Works Cities** is Bloomberg-funded data-practice certification ([results4america.org](https://results4america.org/page/economic-mobility-catalog/)). These catalog *interventions and evidence*, never vendors. Model to emulate (evidence-graded catalog UX), not a competitor.

### 1.8 Code for America

- **Government AI Landscape Assessment** (2025; updated edition May 2026): grades all 50 states across Readiness → Piloting → Implementation → Impact with Early/Developing/Established/Advanced rubrics; finds most states early, measurement underdeveloped, experimentation decentralized ([codeforamerica.org/explore/government-ai-landscape-assessment](https://codeforamerica.org/explore/government-ai-landscape-assessment/)). **Explicitly contains no vendor evaluation or procurement tools.**
- AI-in-benefits build work: **AI Studio**, Medicaid AI use-case identification (data extraction/pre-population, entity resolution, caseworker burden reduction; Jan 21, 2026 — [codeforamerica.org/news/identifying-use-cases-for-ai-in-medicaid-delivery](https://codeforamerica.org/news/identifying-use-cases-for-ai-in-medicaid-delivery/)). CfA builds *with* agencies; it does not vet vendors. Complementary — and its landscape assessment is citable evidence that states lack evaluation capacity.

### 1.9 InnovateUS / GovLab / Burnes Center

- **InnovateUS** (Burnes Center for Social Change at Northeastern, Beth Simone Noveck's ecosystem, with AI for Impact, Reboot Democracy, and The GovLab — which now lives at Northeastern, [thegovlab.org](https://thegovlab.org/)): free at-scale workshops for public servants; Fall 2026 series includes **"AI for Public-Sector Procurement"** and **"Democratic and Public AI: Practical Strategies for Buying, Building, and Governing AI"** plus recorded "AI and Human Services" sessions ([innovate-us.org/series](https://innovate-us.org/series)). Also runs the HHS-leader AI training series in partnership with the Center for Civic Futures ([CCF announcement](https://www.centerforcivicfutures.org/resources/stronger-together-a-new-chapter-for-our-work)). Training, not tooling. Prime distribution partner.

### 1.10 Georgetown Beeck Center

- Runs the **Digital Benefits Network**, **Digital Service Network (8,000+ practitioners)**, State CDO Network, and hosts the **Digital Government Hub** — which already catalogs the GovAI Coalition templates ([digitalgovernmenthub.org](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/library/govai-coalition-templates-and-resources/)) and AI-in-benefits policy resources (e.g., the 2024 HHS plan for responsible AI in public benefits administration — [hub search](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/?s=artificial+intelligence)). Has an "AI Innovation + Incubation Fellow." No vendor-vetting tool. Natural cataloger/amplifier of ours.

### 1.11 Stanford RegLab

- Dan Ho's lab; agency-academic partnerships on AI evaluation: Colorado Dept. of Labor AI adjudication tool (an AI in Action Award winner), regulatory-code mapping for NY/CA/MD, and the influential "Hallucination-Free?" benchmark of commercial AI legal research tools ([reglab.stanford.edu](https://reglab.stanford.edu/)). Rigorous *post-hoc* evaluation of specific deployed systems — the opposite end of the funnel from triage. Methodological ally; a citable model for "verify vendor accuracy claims independently."

### 1.12 Generic AI-vendor due-diligence frameworks (2024–2026)

- Cloud Security Alliance **AI Controls Matrix (AICM) v1.0** (July 10, 2025): 243 control objectives across 18 domains, mapped to NIST AI 600-1, ISO/IEC 42001, and the EU AI Act; companion **AI-CAIQ** questionnaire; **STAR for AI** assurance registry (Oct 23, 2025) ([overview via Atlas Systems](https://www.atlassystems.com/blog/ai-vendor-risk-questionnaire)); FAIR Institute's **FAIR-AIR** loss-exposure approach; plus a wave of consultant checklists ([GLACIS](https://www.glacis.io/guide-ai-vendor-due-diligence), [Ampcus Cyber](https://www.ampcuscyber.com/blogs/third-party-ai-vendor-due-diligence-questionnaire/), etc.). All enterprise-oriented, staff-intensive, vendor-cooperation-dependent, and not written for non-technical government program staff. Raw material for our question banks, not competitors.
- **AI Procurement Lab** (women-led 501(c)3, Lewes, DE): training/certification, AI procurement archetypes, alignment with IEEE P3119 and ISO/IEC JTC1 ([aiprocurementlab.org](https://www.aiprocurementlab.org/)). Consulting + training, not automated tooling.
- **FAS/EPIC contract research** (Kim & Kesari, June 8, 2026): analyzed 215 state AI contracts / 3,771 provisions in CA, UT, FL — **77% boilerplate; only 5.3% address transparency, 3.0% cybersecurity, 2.4% fairness/accountability; >80% of state AI contracts flow through NASPO ValuePoint cooperative agreements**; recommends risk-tiered review modeled on San José and structured vendor AI factsheets as a contract condition ([fas.org](https://fas.org/publication/how-governments-purchase-ai-fair/)). This is the single best citable evidence that the status quo fails.
- Adjacent verification registries our tool should query as legitimacy signals: **GovRAMP** (renamed from StateRAMP Feb 14, 2025; new Core Status tier of 60 controls for smaller vendors, May 2025; GenAI Significant Change/Self-Reporting Addendum and NIST-aligned AI Overlay in progress; 2026 modernization/national adoption push — [govramp.org](https://govramp.org/news/govramp-advances-2026-modernization-and-national-adoption), [Secureframe guide](https://secureframe.com/blog/govramp)); **Pavilion** cooperative-contract search ([withpavilion.com](https://www.withpavilion.com/associations/gov-ai)); SAM.gov and state vendor registrations; FedRAMP marketplace.
- Vendor-side mirror image (useful for positioning rhetoric): **NationGraph** and **Starbridge** sell AI that mines government documents to help *vendors* target agencies, and e.Republic relaunched an "AI-driven Industry Navigator" for companies selling into the public sector ([GovTech, "The New Rules of Procurement," 2026](https://www.govtech.com/budget-finance/the-new-rules-of-procurement-what-it-means-to-buy-tech-in-2026); [e.Republic press release](https://www.einnews.com/pr_news/909770627/e-republic-unveils-ai-driven-transformation-of-industry-navigator-redefining-how-companies-succeed-in-the-public-sector)). **The sell side has AI targeting tools; the buy side has nothing equivalent. Our tool is the counterweight — that asymmetry is the pitch.**

### The gap, stated plainly

Every existing asset fails the core use case in at least one of four ways: (1) **vendor-opt-in** (GovAI FactSheets, marketplaces) — cold-pitch vendors aren't in them; (2) **installed-base-dependent** (Procurated, Govlaunch, G2) — new AI vendors have no reviews; (3) **capacity-intensive** (CSA AICM, NASPO/NIST frameworks, AI Procurement Lab training) — presumes procurement staff time and expertise that the FAS data shows doesn't exist; (4) **wrong funnel stage** (CfA readiness, RegLab evaluation, InnovateUS training). No tool accepts a pitch artifact as input. No tool outputs a graduated recommendation from "could not verify basic legitimacy" to "ask these questions before a demo." The lane is empty.

## 2. Center for Civic Futures (CCF) — what it actually is

- **Identity:** a nonpartisan nonprofit, **fiscally sponsored project of the Digital Harbor Foundation** (Baltimore). It began in **2023 as the Center for Public Sector AI (CPSAI)**; in 2025 CPSAI and the **Public Benefit Innovation Fund (PBIF)** consolidated under the new name "Center for Civic Futures" ([Stronger Together announcement](https://www.centerforcivicfutures.org/resources/stronger-together-a-new-chapter-for-our-work); [digitalgovernmenthub.org directory entry](https://digitalgovernmenthub.org/ecosystem/directory/center-for-civic-futures-ccf/)).
- **Website:** [centerforcivicfutures.org](https://www.centerforcivicfutures.org/) (leadership page: [/leadership](https://www.centerforcivicfutures.org/leadership); LinkedIn: [linkedin.com/company/centerforcivicfutures](https://www.linkedin.com/company/centerforcivicfutures)).
- **Leadership:** Executive Director **Cassandra ("Cass") Madison** (founder **Justin Brown** stepped back to senior advisor/chair of the Public Sector Advisory Board). Team includes Marlena Medford (Sr. Director, Strategic Engagement), Eleanor Davis (Director, PBIF), Jessica Lax (Program Director, State AI Readiness), Leon Wong (CTO in Residence), and Afua Bruce as Distinguished Fellow. Advisory orbit includes Bloomberg Philanthropies, Google DeepMind, Ballmer Group, Gates Foundation, Renaissance Philanthropy ([leadership page](https://www.centerforcivicfutures.org/leadership)).
- **Programs:** (1) **Public Benefit Innovation Fund** — $20M+ for applied AI in public benefits; awarded **$8.5M across 7 projects + a Vals AI SNAP GenAI benchmark** on Dec 9, 2025 (APHSA/Nava multi-state SNAP-Medicaid work verification, Maryland DOL, New Jersey Office of Innovation cross-program eligibility, Mississippi SNAP, NM/OR Public Policy Lab benefit notices, Wisconsin, AffordableHousing.com/Learning Collider), funded by Ballmer, Gates, Kellogg, Families and Workers Fund ([CCF announcement](https://www.centerforcivicfutures.org/resources/center-for-civic-futures-and-partners-commit-8-5m-for-ai-solutions-that-improve-safety-net-program-delivery); [StateScoop](https://statescoop.com/center-civic-futures-state-ai-public-benefit-grants/)). (2) **AI Readiness Project** with The Rockefeller Foundation (announced Nov 4, 2025): expanding a 30+-state community of practice to all 50 states/territories/Tribal Nations by 2026 ([Rockefeller press release](https://www.rockefellerfoundation.org/news/rockefeller-foundation-and-center-for-civic-futures-launch-ai-readiness-project-to-build-state-capacity-for-responsible-ai/)). (3) **AI Knowledge Hub** ([ai-hub.centerforcivicfutures.org](https://ai-hub.centerforcivicfutures.org/)) — **104 state/territory/tribal AI use cases** across 11 application types (chatbots 33, GenAI 29, document processing 27, predictive 18, computer vision 13), naming vendors used (Salesforce, OpenAI, Microsoft, Anthropic, Google) but **with no vendor evaluation, procurement guidance, or pitch triage**. (4) HHS-leader AI training with InnovateUS. (5) AI in Action Awards.
- **Relationship to New America / Ayushi Roy:** **I found no public organizational relationship between CCF and New America, and no public affiliation between Ayushi Roy and CCF.** CCF is Digital Harbor Foundation-sponsored, not a New America entity. Roy was Chief Program Officer of New America's New Practice Lab (~4 years) and was **named State of California CTO on August 20, 2026** (replacing Jonathan Porat, who resigned April 2026); her New America bio page now returns 404 ([StateScoop](https://statescoop.com/california-cto-ayushi-roy/), [GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/workforce/new-california-cto-has-federal-academic-resume)). If the project brief describes CCF and New Practice Lab as co-partners on this tool, that partnership is not yet publicly documented anywhere I could find — worth confirming internally who the CCF point of contact is (most likely Madison, Medford, or Lax given the AI Readiness portfolio).
- **Logo/brand:** **No public press kit or downloadable brand assets found** — centerforcivicfutures.org/press returns 404 and the site nav has no media/brand section. A logo renders on the site and its LinkedIn page, but for co-branding you will need to request files directly from CCF (contact via the site's Contact page). Flag this early; it is on the critical path for the tool's footer.

## 3. New America's New Practice Lab — current workstreams

- A project of New America; "part policy research group, part technology product developers, part pro bono consultancy." Founder & Executive Director **Tara Dawson McGuinness**; Chief of Staff Amira Choueiki Boland; Ayushi Roy was Chief Program Officer until Aug 2026 ([newamerica.org/programs/new-practice-lab](https://www.newamerica.org/programs/new-practice-lab/), [nplimpact.org](https://www.nplimpact.org/)).
- Public workstreams are **paid family & medical leave, early childhood care & education, and tax policy/credits**, delivered through listening research → policy → implementation. Scale claims: 19 state partners; benefits programs touching 2.8M Americans since Jan 2024; 74% of engagements producing real programmatic change ([2025 in Review](https://www.newamerica.org/new-practice-lab/blog/2025-in-review/)).
- Recent publications: 2026 National Parent Survey (May 27, 2026), Virginia paid leave implementation (Apr 23, 2026), user-centered paid leave websites (Jul 13, 2026).
- **The April–May 2026 survey of state benefits agencies (call center ops #1 pitched-and-wanted-evaluated AI use case, then document management and eligibility determination) is not publicly discoverable.** Searches of newamerica.org and the open web found no published version. Treat it as internal/unpublished until confirmed; the tool's public-facing copy should not cite it as a published study without checking. Adjacent public corroboration exists: NJ chief innovation officer Dave Cole's "inboxes are filled with pitches about AI tools" ([StateScoop](https://statescoop.com/states-have-a-chance-to-finally-fix-their-benefits-systems/)) and CfA's Medicaid use-case work showing document/call-center demand.
- Nothing on the public NPL site mentions AI vendor evaluation or procurement — meaning **this tool would be NPL's first public AI/procurement artifact**, and (given Roy's departure to California) institutional continuity for the partnership should be confirmed with McGuinness's team.

## 4. Distribution channels — who would plausibly promote a free triage tool

Ranked by plausibility and reach:

1. **GovAI Coalition (900+ agencies)** — free membership, seven working committees including Vendor Registry and Adoption Support; already distributes free tools through its channels and Substack; ICMA actively drives its membership ([summit page](https://events.govtech.com/GovAI-Coalition-Summit), [ICMA blog](https://icma.org/blog-posts/govai-coalition-free-membership-and-committee-access)). Best-fit partner: our tool operationalizes their FactSheet ideology for the pre-FactSheet stage, and can drive vendors *into* their registry ("no FactSheet on file — ask the vendor to complete one"). A Dec 2026 summit demo is a concrete milestone.
2. **CCF's AI Readiness community (target: all 50 states by end of 2026)** — as a named partner, CCF's state AI leads network and AI Knowledge Hub are the natural first-mile distribution; the Hub could cross-link tool outputs, and the tool can cite Hub deployments as "evidence this vendor's category has worked in government."
3. **ICMA** — demonstrated pattern of promoting exactly this kind of free resource to city/county managers (GovAI blog, podcast, Local Government Reimagined conference).
4. **NASCIO** — has an **Emerging AI Working Group** with sessions on "Leveraging AI in Procurement" and an AI resource collection ([nascio.org resource center](https://www.nascio.org/resource-center/)); state CIOs are the gatekeepers our users escalate to.
5. **NASPO** — >80% of state AI contracts run through NASPO ValuePoint ([FAS](https://fas.org/publication/how-governments-purchase-ai-fair/)), making it the highest-leverage procurement-official channel; site blocked direct fetch (403) so current AI programming unverified — approach via its research/Pulse arm.
6. **Code for America network** — the AI Landscape Assessment team and safety-net portfolio align; CfA convenes state benefits agencies (our exact user).
7. **InnovateUS** — could wrap the tool in a free workshop ("triaging AI vendor pitches") reaching thousands of public servants; already partners with CCF.
8. **Beeck Center Digital Government Hub / Digital Benefits Network** — would catalog the tool alongside GovAI templates; DBN reaches benefits-agency practitioners specifically.
9. **NLC and GFOA** — plausible but weaker: NLC covers municipal AI governance ([nlc.org](https://www.nlc.org/), Aug 18, 2026 feature); GFOA's AI page was unfetchable (403) — **uncertain** what active AI programming exists; both reach generalist local audiences rather than benefits program staff.
10. **Media:** StateScoop and GovTech/e.Republic both cover this beat continuously and covered every launch documented above; a launch story is realistic.

## 5. Positioning implications

- **The lane is empty but the shelf is crowded.** Position as the *missing front door*: "before you open any framework, checklist, or registry, paste the pitch here." Explicitly route outputs to the existing ecosystem (GovAI FactSheet request, GovRAMP status check, Procurated reviews, Pavilion/NASPO contract lookup, InnovateUS training) rather than duplicating any of it — this converts every potential competitor into a partner and evidence source.
- **The evidence layer is the moat.** The tool's checks map to real, queryable registries: GovAI Registry (FactSheet on file?), GovRAMP registry (verified/Core status?), Procurated (reviews from real officials?), Pavilion/cooperative contracts (existing government contracts?), SAM.gov/Secretary of State filings (does the company exist?), CCF AI Knowledge Hub (any documented government deployment?), CfA landscape (state context). Nobody has stitched these together for a non-technical user.
- **The FAS numbers are the opening slide:** 77% boilerplate contracts, 5.3% transparency provisions, sell-side AI tools (NationGraph, Starbridge) optimizing vendor targeting while the buy side triages by inbox. A dam metaphor with data behind it.
- **Never-recommend framing is differentiating and defensible:** every commercial adjacent (Marketplace.city, Civic Marketplace, G2) has a vendor-side revenue conflict; a free, open-source, never-says-buy tool from nonprofits has none — the same "by government, for government" trust posture that made the GovAI Coalition grow from 9 to 900+ agencies in three years.
- **Two loose ends to resolve internally:** (1) confirm the CCF partnership's public articulation and obtain brand assets (no press kit exists); (2) confirm whether the April–May 2026 survey will be published — if yes, launch the tool with it; if not, soften public citations to "a 2026 New America survey of state benefits agencies (forthcoming)."
