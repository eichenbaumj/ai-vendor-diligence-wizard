## 1. planters.com aesthetic — verified teardown and transferable principles

I pulled the live homepage HTML (110KB), its Typekit kit (`https://use.typekit.net/oyw6uzs.css`), and its 2MB theme stylesheet (`https://www.planters.com/wp-content/themes/planters/style.css`) and extracted actual tokens. What the site really is:

**Typography.** Two-tier system with extreme contrast. Display: **Knockout 50/51** (Hoefler's ultra-condensed grotesque; 390 declarations fall back to `Impact, Arial`) set huge, all-caps. Body/UI: **Degular** (273 declarations) via Typekit (kit also loads futura-pt and heebo, unused in the main stylesheet). The premium feel comes almost entirely from *scale contrast in the type* — a loud condensed display face against a quiet humanist body face — not from decoration.

**Color.** A tight, high-frequency token palette, counted from the stylesheet: deep navy `#002e5d` (699 occurrences — the anchor), powder blue `#b9d9eb` (265), butter yellow `#fed141` (193), ice blue `#e3f2fb` (120), cream `#faf5e4` (64), with rare accents `#024b96` and `#e14504`. Sections are **solid color-blocked fields** (navy block → powder-blue block → cream block), not white-page-with-cards. One saturated anchor used generously + two pale tints of it + one cream neutral + one warm accent = the whole system.

**Shape grammar.** One radius rules everything: `border-radius: 100px`/`6.25rem` appears ~180 times — pill buttons, pill chips, pill nav. A single consistent geometric grammar reads as designed; mixed radii read as generic.

**Motion.** Named keyframes tell the story: `home-brand-intro--shello__fade`, `__mrp`, `__wink` — one **choreographed entrance sequence** (the "Shell-o there!" hero: text fades in, Mr. Peanut slides in, winks) — plus `marqueeLTR_Lg`/`marqueeRTL_Sm` **scrolling text marquee bands** used as kinetic texture between sections (component `c-marquee` with a yellow-background variant), and small character animations (`poke`, `tilt`, `wink`, `bounce`). Motion is concentrated in one hero moment and one ambient texture, not sprinkled on every scroll.

**Structure.** Block-based editorial sections, each a full-width "billboard," "callout-gallery," "video-hero," or "marquee" block with a single `h2` headline — magazine spreads, not a grid of cards. Sticky header, mega-menu, one CTA. Notably for §5: planters.com ships a dedicated **print stylesheet** (`print-style.css`).

**Transferable principles for the wizard (mapped to 17A brand):**
1. **Color-block the page in the brand anchor + its tints.** Cobalt `#1F1FD6` as the navy; a pale cobalt tint (~5-10% cobalt on white) and Vellum-type warm neutral as section grounds. Verdict sections get full-bleed color fields, not bordered cards.
2. **Let the serif do the theater.** Source Serif 4 at 64-96px+ for the verdict headline and section openers; Source Sans 3 stays quiet at 16-18px. Personality via type scale, not ornament.
3. **One choreographed moment.** Spend the entire motion budget on the analysis sequence (the 1-3 minute wait) — a staged, narrated intro-style animation — and keep the report itself nearly static.
4. **One radius, everywhere.** Pick pill (100px) for buttons/chips/tier badges and never deviate.
5. **A marquee-style kinetic band** ("Checking registration records… · Reading procurement databases… ·") is a brand-compatible way to make the working state feel alive.

(No agency writeup of the redesign surfaced in search: [designrush listing](https://www.designrush.com/agency/website-design-development/website-redesign) et al. were generic.)

## 2. Best-in-class "submit → analysis" UX, verdict formats, and what non-experts misread

**VirusTotal** ([docs](https://docs.virustotal.com/docs/results-reports)). Detection ratio ("X/72 engines flagged this"), tabbed report (Detection / Details / Relations / Behavior / Community), community score weighted by voter reputation ("Negative (red) scores indicate maliciousness… positive (green) reflect harmlessness"). Docs explicitly warn the ratio must not be read in isolation ([beginner guide](https://chamindux.medium.com/virustotal-101-a-beginners-guide-to-file-analysis-and-threat-detection-46f512f39578)). **Lesson:** the consensus-of-independent-checks framing ("6 of 40 sources raised flags") is legible to lay users; per-engine tri-state verdicts (flagged / clean / timeout) model honest per-check reporting. But VT's tab-heavy expert density is the anti-pattern for this audience — keep the evidence, lose the tabs.

**SSL Labs** ([SSL Server Rating Guide](https://github.com/ssllabs/research/wiki/SSL-Server-Rating-Guide)). A+–F letter grades; they **removed the visible numeric score** because "the letter grade (A-F) is more useful." Two mechanics worth stealing: **automatic-F conditions** (one fatal flaw — e.g., Heartbleed — overrides an otherwise fine average) and **grade caps** (a lesser issue caps you at B). For vendor triage: a fabricated address or nonexistent registration should hard-override to "could not verify legitimacy" no matter how polished everything else is — never average a fatal flaw away. Stated philosophy: enable people "to assess SSL server configuration confidently without the need to become SSL experts." SSL Labs also displays a live per-check log during its 1-2 minute scan (widely described, e.g., [Gcore guide](https://gcore.com/learning/how-to-get-a-plus-ssllabs); I'm confident but flagging this as observed behavior, not fetched this session).

**MDN HTTP Observatory** ([scoring methodology](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/observatory/docs/tests_and_scoring)). Baseline 100, round of penalties, then bonuses **only if you already scored 90+** (extra credit can't rescue a failing site — smart). Grade table: A+ =100+, A 90-99 … F 0-24. Refreshingly honest: "the letter grades are essentially arbitrary" but tuned by expert feedback, and "what may be unnecessary for one site… might mitigate important risks for another" — context-dependence language worth echoing.

**Have I Been Pwned** ([haveibeenpwned.com](https://haveibeenpwned.com/)). The gold standard of lay-legible verdicts: one input, one button, instant plain-spoken verdict — "**Good news — no pwnage found!**" vs "**Oh no — pwned!**" — followed immediately by an evidence table (which breaches, when). Legitimacy signaled by scale stats ("17,795,083,974 pwned addresses"). **Lesson:** verdict-first in human words, evidence directly beneath, warm non-jargon tone.

**mail-tester.com**. Core flow (fetched): "**Test the Spammyness of your Emails — First, send your email to:** [generated address]" then a score out of 10; findings expand in accordion sections by check category (accordion detail from memory — flagged). Notable for accepting an *artifact the user already has* (an email) as the input — the same move as pasting a vendor pitch.

**Hemingway** ([readability checker](https://hemingwayapp.com/readability-checker)). Verdict as a **school grade level** — "the average level of education needed for an adult to understand your text," target 8th grade because that's the average US adult reading level — with inline color-highlighted evidence (yellow = 4+ grades above average adult level, red = 6+). **Lesson:** verdicts anchored to a familiar real-world scale + evidence highlighted in situ on the user's own submitted text. Highlighting risky claims inside the pasted pitch itself would be a distinctive, powerful feature.

**Jobscan** ([resume checker](https://www.jobscan.co/resume-checker)). Match rate 0-100% with an explicit action threshold ("at least 75%"), "over 30 checks" grouped into categories (missing keywords, formatting flags, recruiter tips), and a **fix → re-scan loop**. **Lesson:** publish the number of checks run (concrete thoroughness), group findings by category, and support re-running (e.g., "vendor answered the questions — paste their response").

**NewsGuard** ([rating process](https://www.newsguardtech.com/ratings/rating-process-criteria/)) — the closest structural analog to vendor triage. Nine weighted **binary** criteria (e.g., "Does not publish false… content" 22 pts; "Website discloses ownership and financing" 7.5 pts) summing to 100, mapped to **plain-language action tiers**: 75-99 "Generally Credible," 60-74 "Credible with Exceptions," 40-59 "**Proceed with Caution**," 0-39 "Proceed with Maximum Caution." Each rating is a "Nutrition Label": per-criterion pass/fail **plus written explanations with supporting evidence**. Un-scorable site types (satire, platforms) get **alternative designations instead of scores** — the model for "could not verify basic legitimacy."

**BBB** ([overview of ratings](https://www.bbb.org/overview-of-bbb-ratings)). A+–F based on obtainable information; **"NR" (No Rating)** for "insufficient information about a business" — an explicit insufficient-evidence state; and a standing disclaimer that the rating "is not a guarantee of a business's reliability or performance." A triage tool needs exactly this disclaimer posture (it never says "buy this").

**Charity Navigator** ([methodology](https://www.charitynavigator.org/about-us/our-methodology/)) rates "across the four beacons that comprise our Encompass Rating System" — multi-dimension beacons rather than one flattening score (beacon names and not-enough-data details are on subpages I could not fetch; flagged).

### What non-experts misread — research
- **Simplified numeric-facts labels fail:** in a PLOS One front-of-package study, "accuracy was *below* chance on half of the comparisons despite consulting FOPs" (38% on cereal comparisons); more label information didn't guarantee better decisions, and literacy still dominated ([PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0125306)).
- **Label complexity has a sweet spot:** CHI 2024 IoT-label study with 518 purchasers tested low (QR code + Cyber Trust Mark only), medium, high, and ultra-high complexity labels — the design space explicitly assumes QR-only is too little and expert-grade labels too much ([CHI 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3613904.3642011); full-text was paywalled, characterization is from the abstract/summary — flagged).
- **Layered disclosure is the research-backed pattern:** Emami-Naeini et al.'s IoT label puts "the most critical factors… on the primary layer" with 47 factors total on a secondary layer ([Ask the Experts, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.04631); [attributes → risk perception, IEEE S&P 2021](https://www.iotsecurityprivacy.org/downloads/Emami-Naeini_Oakland21_IoTLabel-WhichAttributes.pdf)).
- **Security icons/strings confuse non-experts:** Felt et al. surveyed 1,329 people and evaluated 40 icons and 7 strings before Chrome adopted three redesigned indicators ([Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/rethinking-connection-security-indicators/)) — evidence that even single-glyph "verdicts" get misread without words attached.
- **Raw percentages mislead novices:** Google PAIR: "Novice users may not know whether a value like 80% is low or high," and "If the confidence level could be misleading for less-savvy users… reconsider… whether to display it at all" ([PAIR Guidebook](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)).
- **Practitioner convergence on letter grades over numbers** (SSL Labs dropped its numeric display), but letter grades still needed BBB-style "not a guarantee" disclaimers, and school-grade connotations invite "we bought the A vendor" misuse — which this tool must never enable.

**Verdict-format recommendation:** 3-5 **named action tiers in plain sentences** (NewsGuard-style: "Established vendor — ask these questions before a demo" / "Proceed with caution — gaps we found" / "Could not verify basic legitimacy — do not engage"), each with a fixed color + icon + label (never color alone — WCAG 1.4.1), a per-check evidence panel underneath, an SSL-Labs-style fatal-flaw override, and **no visible 0-100 score** and no purchase-grade letter.

## 3. Trust-communication patterns

- **Confidence tiers:** PAIR: categorical High/Medium/Low buckets require "careful cutoff point selection and clear user action guidance for each category"; in low-confidence situations, "showing multiple options prompts the user to rely on their own judgement" ([PAIR](https://pair.withgoogle.com/chapter/explainability-trust/)). Tie every confidence tier to a next step, e.g., "Medium confidence — verify the state registration yourself at [link]."
- **Set expectations up front:** Microsoft's Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction — G1 "make clear what the system can do," G2 "make clear how well the system can do what it can do" — with guidance staged "upon initial interaction, during interaction, when the system is wrong, and over time" ([HAX Toolkit](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/ai-guidelines/)). Concretely: a permanent one-liner under the verdict — "This tool checks public evidence about vendors. It does not test their products and never recommends purchases."
- **Evidence citations:** NewsGuard's per-criterion written explanations with evidence; HIBP's breach table directly under the verdict; VirusTotal's per-engine attributions. Every claim in the report should carry a named source with URL and retrieval date — which also makes the printed report defensible in a procurement file.
- **"What we checked / what we could not check":** the strongest honesty pattern found across tools — VirusTotal's per-engine "Undetected/Timeout" states, BBB's "NR," NewsGuard's alternative designations for unscoreable sites, Observatory's context-dependence caveat. Build a fixed **checklist panel of every check the tool always attempts**, each resolving to pass / flag / **"could not check (why)"** — an unchecked item shown as unchecked prevents false assurance and is itself a triage signal ("no state registration found" ≠ "registration OK").
- **Timestamp everything** ("checked Aug 28, 2026") and offer a re-run — stale verdicts on fast-moving AI vendors are a liability (re-analyze pattern per VirusTotal; flagged as product observation).

## 4. Long-running task UX (1-3 minutes): staged progress storytelling

- **Thresholds:** Nielsen's limits — 0.1s feels instant, 1.0s keeps flow, 10s is the attention limit; beyond 10s, "percent-done progress indicators should be used," plus "a clearly signposted way for the user to interrupt" ([NN/g response times](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/)). Spinners only for 2-10s; for long multi-step waits show **step counts** ("Users might not know how long each step lasts, but knowing the number of steps at least helps them form an estimate") paired with explanatory text ([NN/g progress indicators](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progress-indicators/)).
- **The labor illusion:** Buell & Norton (Management Science 2011): when sites show the work (searched sites, intermediate results), "people can actually prefer websites with longer waits to those that return instantaneous results — even when those results are identical"; effort perception → reciprocity → higher valuation ([paper](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mnsc.1110.1376); [HBS PDF](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/Norton_Michael_The%20labor%20illusion%20How%20operational_f4269b70-3732-4fc4-8113-72d0c47533e0.pdf)). A 1-3 minute analysis is a trust asset if narrated. Follow-on work, "Surfacing the Submerged State: Operational Transparency in Government Services" (Buell, Porter, Norton; working paper 2014, later published in M&SOM), extends this to trust in government specifically (existence verified via [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works?query.title=Surfacing+the+Submerged+State); findings characterization from memory — flagged).
- **Named examples done well:** SSL Labs' live scrolling check log during its ~2-minute scan; Kayak-style "searching 500 airlines" progressive result feeds (the actual stimulus family in Buell & Norton's experiments); Domino's Pizza Tracker and TurboTax's animated "checking your return" review (canonical practitioner examples — flagged as product observations); growth.design's ["Labor Perception Bias: Why faster isn't always better"](https://growth.design/case-studies/labor-perception-bias) case study; AI deep-research products (Perplexity, ChatGPT deep research) streaming their step-by-step research plan and sources (product observation — flagged).
- **Gov-familiar component:** USWDS **step indicator** — "updates users on their progress through a multi-step process," variants with counters, `aria-current="true"` on the current step, "[step] of [total]" ([USWDS](https://designsystem.digital.gov/components/step-indicator/)).
- **Recommended pattern:** 4-6 **named stages in plain language** ("Reading the pitch → Identifying the company → Checking registrations & records → Looking for delivery evidence → Writing your report"), each streaming its own micro-findings as they land ("✓ Found Delaware registration, 2024") — the findings *are* the progress bar; overall step count + a determinate bar that never stalls; `aria-live="polite"` announcements (WCAG 4.1.3); a cancel affordance; and the planters-style marquee band as the ambient working texture.

## 5. Government-audience constraints

- **Accessibility is now legally loaded for this exact audience:** DOJ's ADA Title II web rule requires state/local government web content to meet **WCAG 2.1 Level AA** — compliance deadlines **April 26, 2027** (entities ≥50,000 population) and **April 26, 2028** (<50,000 and special districts) ([ada.gov](https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-rule-first-steps/)). A tool marketed to these agencies should itself be demonstrably 2.1 AA and say so.
- **AA essentials for a form + report tool** ([WCAG at a glance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/glance/)): full keyboard operability, visible focus, error identification, status messages (progress updates via `aria-live`, not visual-only), text alternatives, and contrast. Verified brand math: cobalt `#1F1FD6` on white = **9.44:1** and on warm cream `#FAF5E4` = **8.65:1** (both pass AA/AAA); white on cobalt = 9.44:1; charcoal `#3B3B3B` on white = 11.2:1; but Carolina Blue `#21A8E0` on white = **2.71:1 — fails AA for text**; keep it to large decorative use only. Never encode verdict tiers by color alone.
- **Plain language:** the Plain Writing Act of 2010 requires public-facing content "written for its specific audience"; concrete rules from the federal guide: "Active voice makes it clear who should do what," "The present tense makes your writing simpler, more direct, and more forceful," shorter words and short sections, address the reader as "you" ([digital.gov plain-language guide](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language), [writing guide](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/writing)). Target ~8th-grade reading level for verdict copy (the average US adult level, per [Hemingway](https://hemingwayapp.com/readability-checker)). Run the tool's own output through a readability check in CI.
- **Printable/PDF report:** procurement runs on files-in-folders and email attachments. Ship a real `@media print` stylesheet (even planters.com ships `print-style.css`): single column, serif headings survive, accordions/`details` force-expanded, tier colors replaced by labels + borders (print may be B&W), every cited URL printed in full as a footnote, timestamp + "what we could not check" always included, letter-size margins. Browser print-to-PDF is the zero-dependency path; a "Download PDF" button can simply invoke it.
- **Locked-down old browsers:** USWDS's policy is instructive — supports browsers >2% usage per [analytics.usa.gov](https://analytics.usa.gov/), dropped IE11 at v3.0, and builds on progressive enhancement: "If JavaScript fails users will still get a robust HTML foundation and all the necessary content" ([USWDS developers doc](https://designsystem.digital.gov/documentation/developers/)). Practical floor: agency machines often run IT-frozen Chrome/Edge several versions back. Server-render the report page as plain semantic HTML (also what makes print work); treat streaming progress as an enhancement with a meta-refresh or polling fallback; avoid bleeding-edge CSS without fallbacks; self-host fonts (some agencies block third-party font CDNs — practitioner knowledge, flagged).

## 6. Editorial (non-dashboard) reference sites — verified typography

All font stacks below were verified by fetching each site's live CSS this session. All five are civic/institutional and compatible with Source Serif 4 headings + Source Sans 3 body + cobalt + warm neutrals.

1. **The Markup** ([themarkup.org](https://themarkup.org)) — Ivar Display (serif) headlines, Messina Sans body, Roboto Mono for data accents (verified in `main.*.css`). Tech-accountability journalism: serious data findings presented as magazine features with generous whitespace. The closest aesthetic north star: serif display + sans body + mono for evidence is exactly the wizard's report grammar.
2. **The Marshall Project** ([themarshallproject.org](https://www.themarshallproject.org)) — Miller (Scotch Roman serif) display + Acumin Pro sans + Utopia via Typekit (verified, kit `crx5tsx`). Nonprofit criminal-justice newsroom: restrained palette, findings-first headlines, institutional gravitas without dashboard chrome.
3. **Vital City** ([vitalcitynyc.org](https://www.vitalcitynyc.org)) — Halyard Display/Text/Micro via Typekit (verified, kit `nlh6iak`). A civic policy journal ("pragmatic ideas to solve cities' hardest problems") that embeds interactive crime-data explorers *inside* an editorial frame — the best mission-analog: rigorous public-sector data wearing a journal's clothes, not a BI tool's.
4. **Works in Progress** ([worksinprogress.co](https://worksinprogress.co)) — Editor (serif) + GT America Mono, warm off-white ground `#fff7f4`, near-black ink `#121212`, muted per-issue accent palette (all verified in `Layout.*.css`). The best model for **warm-neutral editorial color handling** and for making long analytical prose feel premium.
5. **ProPublica** ([propublica.org](https://www.propublica.org)) — tokenized type system `--p-fonts-hed` / `--p-fonts-sans` / `--p-fonts-serif` (verified in markup/CSS). Investigative nonprofit whose article furniture (methodology boxes, "How we did this" sidebars, muted link styling) maps directly onto the wizard's evidence/transparency panels.

Context notes: partner **New America** uses Space Grotesk ([newamerica.org](https://www.newamerica.org), verified) — no need to match, but avoid clashing when co-branded. And planters.com itself (§1) demonstrates the same underlying grammar — big display type, color-blocked sections, one radius, choreographed motion — that these editorial sites use in quieter registers.
